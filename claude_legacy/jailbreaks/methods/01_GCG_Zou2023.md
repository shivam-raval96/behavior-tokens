## Problem Statement

Aligned LLMs are trained via RLHF to refuse harmful requests. Prior jailbreak methods either required
significant **manual effort** (hand-crafted prompts) or were **ineffective** at automating adversarial suffix
discovery. The paper asks: can we automatically and reliably find adversarial suffixes that force aligned
models to comply, and do those suffixes generalize across models and behaviors?

---

## Core Idea

Append an **adversarial suffix** of $k$ discrete tokens to a harmful query and optimize it so the model's
response begins with an affirmative prefix (e.g., `"Sure, here is how to..."`).

The key insight is to treat this as a **loss minimization problem over discrete tokens**, solved via a
greedy coordinate descent on the token space using gradient information.

```
[Harmful query] + [adversarial suffix tokens] → LLM → "Sure, here is..."
```

---

## Method: Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG)

### Algorithm (Single Behavior / Single Model)

1. **Initialize** suffix $s$ of length $l$ with random tokens.
2. **For** each iteration $t = 1 \ldots T$:
   - Compute gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{e}_{s_i}} \mathcal{L}$ for each suffix token position $i$, where
     $\mathbf{e}$ is the one-hot embedding of token $s_i$. This gives a linear approximation of which
     tokens would most reduce the loss if substituted.
   - For each position $i$, take the **top-$k$ token candidates** ranked by negative gradient.
   - **Sample** a random batch of $B$ candidate (position, token) swaps from the top-$k$ candidates
     across all $l$ positions.
   - For each candidate swap, compute the **actual loss** via a forward pass.
   - **Keep** the candidate with the lowest loss. Replace the current suffix token at that position.
3. Return the optimized suffix.

### Algorithm (Universal / Multi-Model)

- Runs the same loop but accumulates the loss across **multiple harmful behaviors** and **multiple models**
  simultaneously, finding a single suffix that generalizes.
- Two open-source models (Vicuna + Guanaco variants) used during optimization; the resulting suffix is
  then evaluated for transfer to black-box models.

### Loss Function

$$\mathcal{L}(x_{1:n}) = -\log p(x^*_{n+1:n+H} \mid x_{1:n})$$

Where $x^*_{n+1:n+H}$ is the target affirmative prefix (e.g., `"Sure, here is"`).

### Hyperparameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Suffix length $l$ | 20 tokens |
| Top-$k$ candidates per position | 256 |
| Batch size $B$ | 512 |
| Optimization steps $T$ | 500 |
| Target prefix | `"Sure, here is how to [behavior]"` |

## Datasets

### Harmful Behaviors
- **AdvBench** (introduced by this paper): 520 harmful instruction-following tasks covering synthesis of
  dangerous substances, cyberattacks, disinformation, harassment, and more.
- Used 100 behaviors for single-model evaluation; 25 behaviors for universal multi-model optimization.

### Harmful Strings
- 100 specific harmful output strings the model must produce verbatim (e.g., exact sentences from
  dangerous content). Harder than Harmful Behaviors — requires exact match, not just topic compliance.

## Key Observations

1. **Gradient approximation works for discrete tokens.** Despite tokens being discrete, the linear
   approximation via one-hot gradients gives sufficient signal for effective optimization.

2. **Universality.** A *single* suffix can simultaneously jailbreak a broad range of harmful behaviors
   without per-behavior tuning.

3. **Transferability.** Suffixes optimized on open-source models transfer to closed-source models with
   surprisingly high success — even models they've never seen.

4. **Claude is the hardest target.** Claude-2's ASR was only 2.1%, consistent with its more extensive
   safety training and RLHF approach.

5. **Nonsensical suffixes.** GCG produces semantically meaningless token sequences (e.g.,
   `"describing.-- !!! ! format 실`). These have very high perplexity and are detectable by simple
   perplexity-based filters.

6. **Adversarial examples follow a similar pattern to vision models.** The existence of universal,
   transferable adversarial attacks raises the same fundamental questions about the robustness of
   alignment that adversarial examples raised for image classifiers.

7. **ASR decreases with model robustness.** More heavily aligned models (LLaMA-2-Chat) are harder to
   attack than lightly aligned ones (Vicuna), but still vulnerable.

---

## Limitations

- **White-box access required** for optimization.
- **Computationally expensive:** ~500 forward+backward passes per attack.
- Produced suffixes have **very high perplexity** — trivially detectable by perplexity filters.
- **Black-box transfer is inconsistent**: some models (Claude-2) are nearly immune; transfer ASR
  depends heavily on how similar the target model's training was to the surrogate.
- Evaluated with string-matching-based judge (checking for refusal phrases), which may **overestimate** ASR.

---

## Influence and Legacy

- 2,200+ citations as of 2025.
- Established **AdvBench** as the standard jailbreak evaluation benchmark.
- Introduced the term "adversarial suffix" to the LLM safety literature.
- Spawned a large family of follow-up work: GCG++, I-GCG, MAC, SM-GCG, AmpleGCG, Mask-GCG, AutoDAN.
- Catalyzed research into **defenses** (perplexity filtering, smoothing, certified defenses).
- Released `nanogcg` (2024) — a fast pip-installable implementation of GCG.

## Our Validation (this repo)

Reproduced stock GCG on **Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct** — impl [../gcg_bench/](../gcg_bench/),
numbers in [../RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md).

- **Llama-2-7B-Chat, AdvBench 100 beh, PAPER-EXACT (500 steps, batch 512, suflen 20):**
  ASR **0.34** vs 0.01 baseline. The paper's **0.88** on the same model does **not**
  reproduce here — and it is NOT a judge artifact (paper-style english-prefix matcher
  gives 0.35 vs our strict 0.34, identical). Likely causes: mirror-synthesized (awkward)
  target strings vs the paper's real AdvBench targets, plus the known difficulty of
  reproducing the 88% (HarmBench/nanoGCG report far lower under uniform eval). Full
  analysis in [../RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md).
- **Llama-2-7B-Chat, jbb, 10 beh, 250 steps:** ASR **0.50** vs 0.00 baseline (A100 fan-out, 10.5 min).
- **Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, jbb, 10 behaviors, 250 steps:** ASR **0.30** vs **0.20** baseline (A10G, ~36 min).
- Confirms limitation #189 empirically: refusal-string matching **over-estimated** ASR
  at 0.50 — GCG suffixes flip the 1B model into refusing *in another language*
  (French / Indonesian / Chinese), which an English-only filter scores as success.
  Added a non-Latin-script guard + multilingual stems ([../gcg_bench/asr.py](../gcg_bench/asr.py)),
  re-scored offline → **0.30**.
- Suffixes are high-perplexity gibberish, matching observation #169.
